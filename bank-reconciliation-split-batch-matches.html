Bank reconciliation explained: timing differences, split payments and batch deposits

2026-09-20

A bank reconciliation compares two records of the same money: what the bank says happened, and what your books say happened. They rarely agree on any given day. The job is to explain every difference, and to find the ones that are real errors rather than timing.

The standard formula

You reconcile both sides to an adjusted balance, and the two adjusted balances should be equal:

  • Bank side: balance per bank statement + deposits in transit - outstanding (uncleared) cheques and payments ± any bank errors = adjusted bank balance
  • Books side: balance per books + interest and other credits the bank has recorded - fees and charges the bank has recorded ± any book errors = adjusted book balance

If the two adjusted figures differ, something is unexplained. Do not "plug" the difference; find it.

The reconciling items you will meet most

Deposits in transit. You banked a payment on the last day of the month and recorded it in your books, but it appears on the bank statement next month. Timing only. No entry needed.

Outstanding cheques and payments. You wrote and recorded a cheque, but the supplier has not cashed it yet. Timing only. Check it clears next month, and chase it if it stays open for weeks.

Bank fees and interest. The bank recorded them; you have not. These need a journal entry in your books.

Errors. A transposed number (1,250 entered as 1,520), a payment recorded twice, or one recorded in the wrong account. These need a correcting entry.

Matches that are not one-to-one

A simple matcher pairs one bank line with one book line of the same amount. Real data is messier in two common ways.

Split (one bank line, several book lines). A customer pays 3,450.00 in one transfer covering two invoices, INV-1041 (1,200.00) and INV-1042 (2,250.00). The bank shows one line; your books show two. Individually neither of your lines matches the bank, but together they do.

Batch (several bank lines, one book line). A card processor pays out two settlements, 500.00 and 400.00, on consecutive days. Your books show a single 900.00 deposit. Again neither bank line matches alone.

The safe way to handle both: only combine a small number of lines (two or three), only within a short date window, and require the sum to match to the cent. Anything that cannot be matched that strictly should stay unmatched and be looked at by a person.

Possible duplicates

Two identical charges on the same day from the same supplier might be a duplicate charge, or two genuine subscriptions. The data alone cannot settle it. Flag it, check the supplier's invoice, and ask for a refund only if it really is a duplicate.

A month-end habit that works

  1. Reconcile every account, every month, before you produce any reports.
  2. Explain every unmatched line with a reason and an action, not just a total.
  3. Keep a short list of open items (uncleared cheques, queries to clients) that carries forward.
  4. Have someone other than the preparer review it.
